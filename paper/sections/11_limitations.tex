\section{Limitations and Open Questions}
\label{sec:limitations}

\subsection{Algorithmic Limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Combinatorial decay of $P_{\mathrm{CF}}$:} Even with variational optimization, $P_{\mathrm{CF}}$ decays with $n$. For $n=12$, $P_{\mathrm{CF}} \approx 0.09$ on path graphs, requiring $\sim$11 shots per collision-free event. This limits practical problem size to $n \lesssim 12$ without error correction or loss mitigation.
\item \textbf{Optimization landscape:} The $P_{\mathrm{success}}$ landscape is non-convex with many local optima. COBYLA with random restarts works for $n \le 12$ but may struggle at larger $n$. Gradient-based optimization (via parameter-shift rules or adjoint methods) could improve scalability.
\item \textbf{Optimum oracle dependence:} $P_{\mathrm{success}}$ requires knowing $C^*$ (the optimal energy). In practice, one can use a lower bound or the best energy found so far, but this introduces a feedback loop that needs careful analysis.
\item \textbf{No quantum speedup claim:} We do not claim asymptotic quantum advantage. The advantage is \textbf{constant-factor} at fixed $n$, arising from the physical constraint enforcement vs. soft penalty. Whether this translates to asymptotic advantage is open.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Hardware Limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Photon loss:} Current QPUs have $\eta \approx 0.7$--$0.9$ per mode. Loss reduces $P_{\mathrm{CF}}$ and introduces vacuum terms. Our noise model (Section~\ref{sec:noisy}) shows $P_{\mathrm{success}}$ degrades gracefully but significantly at $\eta=0.9$.
\item \textbf{Partial distinguishability:} Imperfect photon indistinguishability ($\xi < 1$) reduces interference contrast. The bosonic advantage disappears as $\xi \to 0$.
\item \textbf{Detector limitations:} PNR detectors have limited photon-number resolution (typically up to 3--4 photons), dark counts, and finite efficiency. Our simulator assumes ideal PNR.
\item \textbf{Phase noise and drift:} Interferometer phases drift over time, requiring active stabilization or frequent recalibration.
\item \textbf{Source limitations:} Heralded single-photon sources are probabilistic; deterministic quantum dot sources are needed for high repetition rates.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Theoretical Open Questions}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Pareto frontier characterization:} What is the exact achievable region $(P_{\mathrm{CF}}, P_{\mathrm{opt|CF}})$ for optimum-blind optimization? We have numerical evidence but no analytical bounds.
\item \textbf{Bosonic advantage mechanism:} Why does the bosonic advantage emerge at $n\ge 8$ on structured graphs? Is it related to the spectral properties of the graph Laplacian?
\item \textbf{Collision suppression vs. concentration tradeoff:} Can we prove a fundamental bound on $P_{\mathrm{success}}$ for any interferometer, or is the Pareto frontier $(1,1)$ always reachable with an oracle?
\item \textbf{Loss-aware optimization:} Can we derive analytical gradients of $P_{\mathrm{success}}(\eta,\xi)$ for noise-aware optimization?
\item \textbf{Classical simulability:} The variational circuits we use (Clements mesh with $k=n/2$ photons) are classically simulable for $n \le 12$ via exact permanent calculation. At what $n$ does classical simulation become intractable?
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Scope of Claims}

We make the following \textbf{precise claims}:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Physics:} Full Fock space simulation with correct permanent normalization is validated against Perceval (error $< 10^{-15}$).
\item \textbf{Metric:} $P_{\mathrm{success}} = P_{\mathrm{CF}} \times P_{\mathrm{opt|CF}}$ is the correct hardware objective; conditional energy is insufficient.
\item \textbf{Numerical:} Variational optimization on Clements mesh achieves $P_{\mathrm{success}}$ 5$\times$--227$\times$ higher than Haar and 1.1$\times$--10$\times$ higher than penalty at equal budget, for $n=4$--12.
\item \textbf{Bosonic advantage:} Indistinguishable photons outperform distinguishable photons by up to 4.8$\times$ on structured graphs at $n\ge 8$.
\item \textbf{QPU protocol:} A complete, falsifiable protocol is defined for Belenos.
\end{itemize}

We do \textbf{not} claim:
\begin{itemize}
\item Asymptotic quantum advantage or complexity-theoretic separation.
\item Practical advantage on real hardware (loss, distinguishability, noise untested experimentally).
\item Superiority over all classical algorithms (METIS, KaHIP, etc.) on large graphs.
\item That the variational landscape is easy to optimize for $n > 12$.
\end{itemize}
