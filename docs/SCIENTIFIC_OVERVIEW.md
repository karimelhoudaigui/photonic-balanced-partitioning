# Scientific overview

The original goal was an optimum-blind photonic bisection solver with credible physical-cost benefit, followed by QPU validation. The current experiments do not establish that benefit. This repository now delivers a narrower, testable research contribution rather than retaining unsupported superiority claims.

The primary manuscript question is when output density and expected colliding pairs imply a small absolute error for an independent-particle comparator. P33 supplies an exact sufficient theorem, a sharp balanced two-photon example, and a balanced spectator obstruction when only collision probability is controlled. Its ingredients have known precedents and the complete composition's originality is open.

P37 determines different source-size optima for Haar-averaged threshold feasibility per attempt and per photon. It concerns a known ensemble law, independent uniform loss and specific source bounds. It supplies no graph-quality enrichment or fixed-circuit waiting-time theorem.

E05b is the main negative experiment:756 archived trajectories at34816 ideal draws each. Distinguishable final mean success is higher at n4,6,8; every trajectory already finds an optimum during training. Exact first-shot order cannot be reconstructed from batch counts. P21 classical searches, spectral anchors and graph relabeling controls constrain interpretation further, with their distinct budgets disclosed.

A four-mode/two-photon offline pilot makes a future optical calibration experiment concrete. It validates full Fock references and local circuit exports, then analyzes known or unknown attempt denominators correctly. It has no remote submission implementation and no actual QPU measurements.
