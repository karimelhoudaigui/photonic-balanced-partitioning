# P20 — Graph-informed bounded-block proposals around a classical partition

**Decision: NO-GO for the tested constant-size interacting-block route as a quantum optimization mechanism.** The protocol was implemented and executed, then repeated with more training and fresh test seeds. Every selected circuit in both runs set its interaction angle to zero. Its improvement probabilities and accepted gains were therefore exactly those of a classical disjoint-pair product proposal. Genuine two-photon interference can enrich a particular local improvement relative to distinguishable particles, but the explicit example below is matched or beaten by feasible classical proposals.

This decision closes the specified bounded-block construction, not all photonic optimization. No noise or QPU experiment was run. The manuscript remains outside this work scope.

## Question and mechanism change

Can a graph-informed proposal, made by coherently mixing two or three photons near a classical partition, enrich strictly improving exchanges per physical attempt, without using the optimum?

The previous full-mesh/Haar tuning mechanism was replaced with a local-search proposal. A uniformly random feasible start receives either two best improving single swaps, or classical descent to a strict single-swap local minimum. All single-swap deltas are computed and counted. The proposal block selects two or three disjoint occupied/vacant pairs with the smallest single-swap deltas, with seeded randomized tie breaks. This choice uses graph information, not an exact optimum or a target bisection.

The local circuit first rotates matched occupied/vacant modes and then applies a closed interfering 4- or 6-mode unitary. Outside the block, photons are deterministic spectators. At zero interaction, all occupied-column supports are disjoint: the circuit is exactly a feasible classical product sampler with `P_CF=1`. Nonzero interaction is generated by the normalized four-mode Walsh matrix or the Hermitian part of the six-mode Fourier matrix. Complete local Fock distributions are retained; collisions consume an attempt and yield no candidate.

The target is positive accepted cut gain: `max(C(anchor)-C(candidate),0)`, with nonimproving candidates rejected. No experiment in this report uses the global optimum, success-to-optimum labels, or postselection-normalized gain.

## Protocol, controls and costs

The fixed grid uses five product angles `0,pi/8,pi/4,3pi/8,pi/2` and four interaction angles `0,pi/8,pi/4,pi/2`. Bosons and distinguishable particles each receive the same twenty grid candidates. The product control has the same five product angles. Training maximizes mean positive gain divided by total graph edge weight. Parameters are written to `frozen_parameters.json` **before test graphs and labels are constructed**.

Controls are:

1. Distinguishable particles in the same selected circuit.
2. Distinguishable particles with their own training-selected parameters.
3. Separately trained disjoint occupied/vacant product rotations.
4. Uniform feasible sampling within the same 4/6-mode block.
5. A classical correlated batch proposal, choosing between the unchanged block and replacing every selected occupied vertex, each with probability one half.
6. An exact classical replacement of the complete bosonic block distribution, including collisions.
7. Classical best-of-all feasible local states, charged six or twenty local cut evaluations before choosing its proposal.

Each test method receives 64 **synthetic ideal attempts** per case. All collision outcomes count toward that budget. The exact physical improvement probability per attempt and expected accepted cut gain are recorded alongside multinomial count observations. There are **zero real hardware shots**. These are ideal distribution calculations and simulated finite-count measurements, not hardware results.

Exact training is not free hardware optimization: it consumes the recorded objective evaluations, local cut labels and probability calculations. A training block shares its six or twenty cut labels across parameter candidates, because recomputing identical graph cuts should not artificially inflate classical work. Evaluation-only test labels are recorded separately and never fed into a test-time circuit. Each algorithm also pays for the common classical anchor and informed block selection. Records distinguish scored swap deltas, full-cut evaluations, cached/uncached proposal cut evaluations, physical attempt counts and upfront local enumeration.

All six predeclared families are included: path, cycle, degree-three regular, Erdős–Rényi, weighted Erdős–Rényi and planted partition. No graph was rejected for an unfavorable result, disconnection, or an anchor already being optimal. The regular generator is the repository's degree-preserving-switch sampler, **not a uniform random-regular sampler**. Every graph is independently relabeled before random anchor generation. Path/cycle repetitions change labels and initialization, not graph topology; seed holdout must not be described as unseen graph topologies for those families.

## A real interference effect that fails the classical challenge

Consider eight graph vertices and anchor `{0,1,4,5}`. The active occupied modes are `0,1`, active empty modes `2,3`, and the other photons are spectators. Let the only weighted graph edges be

`(0,1,5), (2,3,5), (0,6,1), (1,6,1)`.

The anchor cut is two. Replacing both active occupied vertices with `{2,3}` gives cut zero, while every active single swap breaks both weight-five edges and is worse. The circuit `i*H_4`, with `H_4` the normalized Walsh matrix whose first two columns have equal signs on the two occupied output rows and opposite signs on the two empty output rows, has:

| Proposal | P_CF | Probability of strictly improving full replacement | Expected accepted cut gain |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Indistinguishable photons | 1/2 | 1/4 | 1/2 |
| Distinguishable photons, same U | 3/4 | 1/8 | 1/4 |
| Independent disjoint-pair rotations at pi/4 | 1 | 1/4 | 1/2 |
| Classical correlated batch | 1 | 1/2 | 1 |
| Deterministic classical full batch swap | 1 | 1 | 2 |

This is genuine two-photon interference with a factor-two **unconditioned** improvement enrichment over distinguishable particles. It is not merely favorable postselection. Nevertheless, the independent product proposal already matches its physical improvement rate, and classical correlated proposals improve on it. Knowing which batch improves is unnecessary for the randomized correlated-batch control. The deterministic last row can be scored directly as one classical candidate; it is shown as a local constructive control, not a claim that arbitrary improving batches are known in advance.

The exact probabilities and gains are asserted in `test_genuine_interference_can_enrich_but_classical_controls_match_or_beat`. This counterexample to an overbroad positive inference explains why "bosons beat distinguishable particles" alone is insufficient.

## Constructive obstruction: bounded interacting blocks admit exact classical replacement

**Proposition, direct proof supplied; novelty unassessed and independent review requested.** Suppose every attempted move prepares a known classical anchor, coherently mixes at most `r` single photons in at most `2r` modes, leaves the other modes deterministic, measures, and then updates the anchor according to a classical policy. For fixed `r<=3`, an entirely classical process can reproduce its complete outcome and adaptive search trajectory distribution exactly, including failed collision attempts.

There are `D=binom(3r-1,r)` local Fock outcomes: ten for `r=2` and 56 for `r=3`. Compute each transition amplitude by explicitly summing `r!` routing products and dividing by the square root of the output occupation factorial. This requires `O(D*r*r!)` arithmetic operations, independent of the global graph size. Square amplitudes, retain collisions as explicit failure outcomes, and sample the resulting categorical law. A graph vertex relabeling or an anchor update changes only the mapping from local outcomes to global vertices; the deterministic spectator law is unchanged.

Conditional on the search history, this replacement has the same next-outcome kernel as the physical proposal. Induction over attempted moves therefore gives equality of the complete adaptive trajectory law for any classical update policy and any fixed attempt budget. The graph-scoring and policy work is common to both processes. The replacement introduces a constant-size probability calculation for bounded `r`; it is not a general efficient simulation of growing boson-sampling instances.

The implemented independent routing enumerator agrees with the Ryser simulator for every local grid kernel to maximum absolute probability error `6.661338147750939e-16`. Tests additionally compare complex random block unitaries and Perceval SLOS. The equality of the constructed classical replacement and bosonic test performance is a consequence of this explicit kernel equality, not an independent hardware observation or a novelty claim.

For one attempted move, the replacement can also sample directly from its feasible conditional distribution whenever `P_CF>0`, eliminating collisions at constant block size and increasing its improvement probability from `P(improve)` to `P(improve)/P_CF`. This statement does not assert monotone dominance of all possible adaptive search trajectories after deleting failures; history-dependent policies can change their behavior. Exact reproduction with failures already establishes the bounded-block obstruction.

This proposition excludes circuits which accumulate coherent overlaps among many blocks before measurement. In that case the factorization used above can fail and the effective interacting component can grow.

## Continuation that changes the obstruction

Keep the genuine finite-block interference example as a diagnostic and preserve the two negative optimization runs. Do not spend more tuning or QPU time trying to turn the same bounded-component mechanism into a quantum computational claim. A larger local grid changes constants but not the exact classical replacement.

A materially different continuation would let coherent overlapping gates connect an increasing number of occupied input modes **before measurement**, while keeping graph-informed training blind to optimum labels. Its first discriminating check is the size/structure of the occupied-mode interaction components and the cost of exact or tensor-network classical replacement. Only if that replacement ceases to be cheap is it useful to test whether a finite-shot improvement survives against matched classical feasible search. Such a route remains open and is not established by P20.
