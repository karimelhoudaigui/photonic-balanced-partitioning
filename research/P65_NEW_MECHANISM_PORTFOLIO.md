## What actually changes in route A

For nonnegative adjacency A and lambda>=0 use

    H_G=(A+lambda*(J-I))/max(1,max_degree(A)+lambda*(N-1)),
    U_G=(I+i*t*H_G)*(I-i*t*H_G)^(-1).

This global single-particle matrix is unitary, symmetric and covariant under
graph relabeling. Rational inputs give Q(i) entries. The K-photon input is
the current feasible state, with paid input routing. The output decoder is
the occupied subset only when collision-free; invalid shots remain self-loops
and consume their full attempt/photon budget. There is no topK tie objective
or unbilled preparation of a selected solution.

The B proposal between feasible S,T is |per U[T,S]|²; D is per|U[T,S]|².
They are symmetric, so the SAME inexpensive classical Metropolis rule gives
the SAME target Gibbs measure. Interference can only help the finite-time
proposal dynamics. This attacks a different question from a lower independent
shot mean. Parameter policies depending on the current state cannot silently
reuse the fixed-parameter detailed-balance proof.

## New autonomous scoped obstructions, with sharp limits

The first proof reconstructs ROOT's collision estimate. For a fixed U and
2<=K<=N, a uniform average collision probability at most epsilon over ALL
K-subset inputs implies a phase-adjusted monomial V with

    ||U-V||_F² <= N*(N-1)*epsilon,
    TV(full K-photon output laws) <= min(1,K*sqrt(N*(N-1)*epsilon)).

The proof includes the positive-support matching/finite convex decomposition
of a doubly stochastic q; it relies on no unverified external theorem.
For symmetric U, mixing the associated permutation with its inverse keeps
the classical proposal symmetric and its Metropolis equilibrium correct.
The uniform-average premise is stronger than observing few collisions on
visited states. The estimate is weak, not a veto at moderate collision
rates, not a relative spectral-gap bound and not a matched-wall-time theorem.
Matrix/permutation construction and every classical update remain paid.
For history-adaptive circuits the same uniform bound must hold conditionally
at EVERY history to obtain the finite-T trajectory bound T*delta.

A second counting proof shows that a graph-blind physical proposal under
fresh uniform embeddings of the CURRENT subset is exactly a classical
mixture of uniform r-replacements. Its quantum complexity only selects K+1
move-size weights. Those weights still need paid acquisition/selection.
The global graph-aligned Cayley candidate falls outside that hypothesis;
its physical matrix changes with the embedding. A relabeling that carries
the graph circuit is not a fresh independent embedding.

Finally, permanent Cauchy--Schwarz gives Q_B<=K!*Q_D at common U and the
corresponding Poincare-gap upper ratio. The factorial is not a small bound
at growing K, and no pathwise hitting-time or equal-shot dominance follows.
These are mathematical restrictions on the proposed mechanism, not a
substitute for the original positive-result objective.
