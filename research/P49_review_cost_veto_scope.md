## Which costs are essential, and to which policy?

For the frozen **B training family**, a direct implementation needs graph input,
the declared Fourier/spectral bases and marks/gauge, occupied-mode rotations,
physical circuit construction, B marginal CDFs for every basis being trained,
PIT randomness, all-shot feasible decoding/scoring, and the exact global
training selection. The paid training shots and fresh evaluations remain
charged. A **previously fixed deployment policy** needs its chosen basis/B
CDFs and classical decoding/scoring, with training cost honestly amortized or
charged under a prospective rule; it need not reprepare unchosen bases.
Physical programming, photon generation/losses/readout/latency remain unmeasured.

The following parts of the current common frontend are NOT dependencies of
V, the all-shot training loss or `complete(a,field,...)`: capped8-scan anchor,
all anchor swap cuts/Delta, signed-eigenvector/support roundings, labelled
assignments, zero/greedy/vacuum-PIT searches and the common best Cref. Cref is
used only for retaining the best already-known partition, not for optical
preparation, loss selection or decoder guidance. Those searches are optional
hybrid discoveries. They are paid for this actual pipeline and given to FAST
freely, but they are not compulsory expenses of every B-only policy.

Similarly, D/b4/continuous-phase/sphere PMFs and B-covariance Gaussian
factorization/masks are comparator machinery, not required by B's own PIT.
Detailed numeric diagnostic/audit construction is not all intrinsic algorithm
work. The measured `common_compute` bundles these with useful B setup; raw and
context JSON are already excluded. Therefore the full shared-wall grant is an
explicit envelope for THIS research pipeline, not a lower bound on the minimum
classical cost of implementing the photonic mechanism. Removing these extras
would also remove the expensive Cref currently supplied free to FAST.
