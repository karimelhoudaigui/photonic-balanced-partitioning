## 2. Clear denominators once, without exponential bit growth

Write each entry A_ji=p_ji/q_ji in binary, q_ji>0. Let B be the total input
encoding length, D the PRODUCT of all denominators, and P=D*A. Then P is an
integer nonnegative matrix and log2 D<=B. Each P entry has O(B) bits, and

    C=AᵀA=M/D², M=PᵀP,
    Z=per(C)=per(M)/D^(2K).                                (1)

M entries have O(B+log(m+1)) bits. No least-common-denominator oracle is
needed. Every residual matrix used later is a submatrix of this SAME M;
its coefficients do not acquire accumulated history-dependent denominators.
Nonzero columns imply M_ii>0 and per(M)>0 by the diagonal term. A zero
column instead yields certain coarse FAIL. K=0 returns the vacuum exactly.

Here is a self-contained integer-weight-to0/1 permanent construction. For a
positive integer w with binary digits `1 b1...bs`, make a directed acyclic
path gadget with entrance p and first frontier v0, edge p→v0. For each digit
bk create uk,vk, add v(k−1)→vk and v(k−1)→uk→vk, and add p→vk iff bk=1.
The number of entrance-to-frontier paths obeys N0=1,Nk=2N(k−1)+bk, hence
the gadget has exactly w entrance-to-exit paths using2+2s internal vertices.
There are no parallel edges. This is a deterministic binary construction.
Leading input zeros are discarded; w=0 means no edge. For w=1 there are
only p,v0 and their single path, with no doubling stage.

Regard M as a weighted directed cycle-cover matrix on K original vertices.
Replace each positive edge i→j of weight w by its OWN disjoint path gadget,
attached i→p and vs→j, and add a unit self-loop to every internal vertex.
Zero edges are absent; retain no other original edges. Original diagonal
weights are treated in the same way, attaching back to the original i.

In a cycle cover of the expanded graph, an entered gadget must carry exactly
one path from i to j: internal non-loop edges are acyclic, each cover vertex
has indegree/outdegree one, and only its entrance/exit touch the outside.
An internal vertex not on such a path must use its self-loop; otherwise
following its non-loop path backward forces an original entrance. Contracting
all selected paths therefore gives an original cycle cover, with independently
w choices for every selected weight-w edge. Conversely those choices and
self-loops give exactly one expanded cover. This proves the EXACT identity

    per(H_M)=per(M), H_M a square0/1 matrix.                 (2)

The directed-graph viewpoint only proves the bijection. H_M is fed to the
primary algorithm as a0/1 matrix, or equivalently as the biadjacency matrix
of row/column copies; its internal diagonal ones are valid bipartite edges.
The size is O(K²(1+B+log(m+1))), including integer entries of huge VALUE.
This depends on their bit lengths, not their values. Identically construct
the gadget for each requested minor. Exact support-perfect-matching tests
handle permanent zero; the empty minor has permanent1. Consequently(2)
and the primary leaf give a finite-bit relative estimator for every M minor
and for Z via(1), without invoking a weighted real-arithmetic estimator.

## 3. Exact Gram-permutation decomposition, including all factorials

First sample a permutation sigma with probability

    Pr(sigma)=product_i C_(i,sigma(i))/per(C)
             =product_i M_(i,sigma(i))/per(M).              (3)

Conditional on sigma, choose K output labels j_i INDEPENDENTLY with

    Pr(j_i=j|sigma)=A_ji*A_(j,sigma(i))/C_(i,sigma(i))
                  =P_ji*P_(j,sigma(i))/M_(i,sigma(i)).      (4)

Each denominator used is strictly positive on the matching support; summing
the numerator over j gives that denominator exactly. Return the occupation
counts n_j of the K chosen labels. No collisions are removed.
Here P_n repeats row j exactly n_j times and n! means product_j n_j!;
all occupied CF input factorials are one.
For an ordered label sequence j=(j1,...,jK), summing the joint law gives

    Pr(j)= [product_i P_(j_i,i)]*per(P_j)/per(M).            (5)

If j has occupation n, P_j is a row permutation of P_n. Furthermore
`sum_(j with counts n) product_i P_(j_i,i)=per(P_n)/product_j n_j!`:
every assignment to identical repeated rows is counted n_j! times in the
permanent. Thus the marginal is EXACTLY

    mu_A(n)=per(P_n)^2/[n!*per(M)]
           =per(A_n)^2/[n!*Z].                             (6)

This verifies ROOT's proposed sigma/j decomposition, rather than assuming
independent individual output photons. Their labels become correlated by
sigma. Positivity is essential for(3)–(4); a sign/gauge error invalidates them.
No m>K premise or padded output rows are needed for this construction.

## 4. Approximate weighted matching using only minor estimates

At a residual row/column set I,J choose a fixed next row i. For EVERY j in J,
test exactly whether M_ij>0 and the remaining support admits a perfect matching.
Set infeasible weights to zero. For feasible j the exact conditional weight is
`w_j=M_ij*per(M_(I\i,J\j))`. Estimate that positive minor relatively within
delta and use `w_hat_j=M_ij*p_hat_j`; sample from their normalized weights.
There is at least one feasible j. If all estimates are zero on a bad call,
choose the first feasible j deterministically: no retry or illegal support.
This fallback's possible bias is charged to the primitive failure event.
Every selected residual still has a perfect matching, even after a bad estimate.

At most K(K+1)/2<=K² minor calls occur (empty minors need no estimator).
For any fixed current history on a good batch,

    (1−delta)w_j<=w_hat_j<=(1+delta)w_j,
    TV(normalize(w_hat),normalize(w))<=delta/(1−delta).    (7)

Indeed the l1 numerator is at most
`sum|w_hat−w|+|sum w_hat−sum w|<=2delta*sum w`; divide by
`2*sum w_hat>=2(1−delta)*sum w`. Fresh primitive randomness makes this a
uniform conditional guarantee at each chosen residual. Coupling per stage
gives matching error at most `K*delta/(1−delta)+K²*eta`, before outer coin
caps, when each minor estimate has failure<=eta. This is NOT obtained by
conditioning the whole adaptive experiment on a favorable final event.

For completeness, amplify a base estimate by a median of r=8u+1 independent
trials, u=ceil(log2(1/eta)). If F is the number of failed trials, then
E[3^F]<=(3/2)^r, so Markov gives Pr(F>=r/2)<=(sqrt(3)/2)^r
<=exp(−r/8)<=2^(−u)<=eta; use log(4/3)>=1/4. Comparisons with powers
of2 compute u; there is no ideal-logarithm coin or indefinite repetition.
The primary's hard caps apply to every trial, including internal random bits.
The same procedure estimates Z with separately declared accuracy/confidence.

## 5. Explicit categorical and Bernoulli coins with worst-case caps

For any finite nonnegative rational weight list, clear its denominators by
their PRODUCT, obtaining integers v_j and V=sum v_j. Their bit lengths are
polynomial: there are at most max(K,m) entries and each operand already has
polynomial bits. Each row is computed afresh, not as a product of past
conditional denominators. The weights in(4) can use the integers P_ji*P_jσi
directly. For V=1 the draw is deterministic; otherwise draw ell=ceil(log2 V)
fair bits as an integer U, accept U<V and use integer cumulative intervals.
Each block accepts with probability>=1/2. After b blocks, abort to coarse
FAIL if none succeeded; this adds at most2^(−b) total variation. Accepted
draws have exactly the rational target law. A rational Bernoulli is the
same construction for its numerator/complement weights.

There are at most Ncoin=2K+1 outer draws: K matching rows, K output labels
and one success coin. Set b=ceil(log2(8*Ncoin/epsilon)); union failure<=epsilon/8.
Hence all outer random bits are bounded by Ncoin*b times the largest integer
bit length. No expected-only rejection loop or repeated-until-no-loss draw
appears. Primitive-internal bits are bounded separately by section1's leaf.
Converting its finite dyadic estimate to an explicit rational and multiplying
by M_ij or dividing by D^(2K) remains polynomial in bits, including bad outputs.
For clarity, the published observation energy is between0 and
`n*ceil(log2(n!))`; averaging polynomially many nonnegative observations adds
only polynomial exponent size. Hence the absolute dyadic exponent is
polynomial in n and log(1/a), not merely its compressed encoding length.
Expanding mantissa times2^exponent therefore needs polynomial bits; the
guard output0 is immediate. This closes the compressed-output loophole.

## 6. Restore raw success mass and give one complete error budget

Let delta_Z=rho_Z=epsilon/8. Independently estimate per(M), divide by D^(2K)
and clip the result Z_hat to[0,1]. Because 0<=Z<=1 by the supplied physical
contraction, the good relative event gives `|Z_hat−Z|<=delta_Z*Z<=delta_Z`;
the bad event contributes at most rho_Z in expectation. Use Bernoulli(Z_hat).
On failure return FAIL. On success run sections3–5 with fresh counting coins.
Thus no cost/error is divided by a possibly exponentially small Z.

Use matching delta=epsilon/(16K), eta=epsilon/(8K²). Its non-coin error is
at most epsilon/15+epsilon/8 by(7). All outer coin caps together contribute
at most epsilon/8, including the success Bernoulli. Coupling the success
flags and then occupations bounds the COMPLETE coarse-law TV by

    delta_Z+rho_Z+epsilon/15+epsilon/8+epsilon/8
       =epsilon/2+epsilon/15 < epsilon.                   (8)

This budget includes every counted primitive failure and every computational
coin abort, mapped to FAIL. Count confidence amplification, all O(K²) calls,
expanded gadget dimensions, exact support tests, rational arithmetic, matrix
input, K labels and m output counters are polynomial in B,m,K,1/epsilon.
The output is one valid full-K occupation or FAIL; illegal lower-total
occupations are never produced as conditional samples. Worst-case bounds
hold also on unlucky runs, not just after averaging over the coins.

K=0 is deterministic vacuum. K=1 admits direct exact rational intensity/
success draws. Zero columns give deterministic coarse FAIL; zero output
rows receive no label in(4). Rank deficiency alone causes no zero-support
claim because collisions are retained. No padding repair is required here.
