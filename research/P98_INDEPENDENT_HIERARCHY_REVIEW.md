## 1. Independent coefficient derivation

For a formal point mass at y, |y|=N, write j=|n|. In expanding the truncated product, the coefficient of z^n factors as

    binom(y,n) Σ_(k=0..s−j)(−1)^k binom(N−j,k).

Indeed `binom(y,r)binom(r,n)=binom(y,n)binom(y−n,r−n)` componentwise, and summing the residual multi-index of total k gives binom(N−j,k). For N>s and j≤s, Pascal cancellation gives `(−1)^(s−j)binom(N−j−1,s−j)`. If n is not componentwise≤y the coefficient vanishes. If N≤s the full finite expansion is z^y instead; no negative upper binomial argument is introduced.

After averaging, contributions from original low occupations cancel exactly in q_s−P. Remaining low-sector contributions all have sign `(−1)^(s−j)`; high sectors are −P. Since Q_s(1)=a₀=1, total signed discrepancy is zero. Its positive mass is therefore its half-L1, and summing each positive sector gives exactly

    V_s(N)=Σ_(j≤s, j≡s mod2) binom(N,j)binom(N−j−1,s−j).

Checks by hand: s0 gives1, s1 givesN, s2 gives `(N−1)²`. At s3,N4 a formal point mass has signed coefficients `(−1,4,−6,4)` below its original degree, so positive mass8 agrees with V₃(4). This is a coefficient check, not a physical optimizer instance. Replacing signed half-L1 by the high-sector probability alone would be wrong.

Termwise factorial cancellation gives

    [binom(N,j)binom(N−j−1,s−j)] / binom(N,s+1)
      =(s+1)binom(s,j)/(N−j).

All terms are positive and decrease as N increases above s. At N=s+1 they become binom(s+1,j); their selected parity sum is2^s, including s0. This independently verifies the constant and all sign conventions in the first part of(R1).

## 2. Physical moments and the column bound

Apply `product_j b_j^(r_j)` to the known CF source, d=|r|. Any repeated source annihilation kills that term. Each distinct removed subset I has its own orthogonal hole state, while its routing amplitudes sum to `per(T[r,I])`. Thus the factorial moment is `Σ_(|I|=d)|per(T[r,I])|²`. Dividing by product r_j! converts falling factorials into binomial moments; there is no additional d! or squared divisor. At d2, one repeated output has numerator4 times each source-pair product and divisor2!, giving exactly the P96 bunch mass. d0 and d>K have the declared empty/zero conventions.

For total counts, weighting observed components in a vacuum dilation gives `E z^N=per(I+(z−1)C)` for real z≥0, hence as a finite polynomial identity. Choosing the C term on a subset I forces every outside index of a permutation to be fixed. Therefore the coefficient of `(z−1)^d` is `Σ_(|I|=d)per(C_I)=E binom(N,d)`.

Each Gram permanent is a nonnegative squared bosonic norm. Each permutation product has modulus≤product_(i∈I)c_i by Cauchy–Schwarz. There are d! products, hence `E binom(N,d)≤d!e_d(c)`. Expanding `(Σc_i)^d` retains at least the d! ordered versions of every distinct subset, so `d!e_d(c)≤µ^d`. With d=s+1 this proves the last two inequalities in(R1) with exactly the stated factors. Fixed photon number without CF input is insufficient for this source-subset derivation.

## 4. Arithmetic, denominators and random-bit costs

For each d, the number of moment/subset pairs is `binom(m+d−1,d)binom(K,d)`. Inclusion-exclusion evaluates the d-permanent in O(d2^d) arithmetic. Also `product_j(r_j+1)≤2^d`, so expanding the moment polynomial does not add an unaccounted exponential in m. There are `binom(m+s,s)` low categories. Together these give the stated `(mK)^{O(s)}` construction/storage regime, with trivial zero cases and paid input/setup handled separately.

With fixed µ≤ρ<1/2 and fixed ε>0, s is a constant. With ε inverse-polynomial in explicit input length L and fixed ρ, s=O(log L) and construction is in general `L^{O(log L)}`, not a uniformly polynomial-in-L-and1/ε simulator. As ρ approaches1/2 no fixed exponent is certified. A polynomial number of adaptive steps generally needs inverse-polynomial individual errors and inherits the quasi-polynomial regime.

Put all real/imaginary rational components over a common positive integer D. A d-permanent has Gaussian-integer numerator over D^d, and its squared modulus has integer numerator over D^(2d). The sole additional moment denominator is product r_j!. Since that divides d! and d! divides s!, every moment denominator divides `M=D^(2s)s!`; integer expansion coefficients introduce no new denominator. Hence q_s=k_n/M exactly, and Σk_n=M guarantees `W=Σmax(k_n,0)≥M≥1`. The repaired weights are exactly max(k_n,0)/W.

Common-denominator formation has bit length bounded by the input encoding length. Products, factorials, subset sums and binomial expansion add at most polynomially many bits in s, that length and logarithmic dimensions; the exponential NUMBER of operations/categories is already counted separately. Thus the exact rational implementation claim does not hide an exponential bit size in a unit-cost denominator. This statement assumes ordinary explicit rational input and encoded accuracy, not a free arbitrary-real oracle or a certified floating comparison near zero.

For W>1 use the exact integer value `b=bit_length(W−1)`, equivalent to ceil(log₂W). A b-bit proposal is accepted with probability W/2^b≥1/2. Conditional on acceptance, each integer below W is equally likely, so cumulative integer weights give r_s exactly. Expected proposal blocks≤2 is not a worst-case wall-time guarantee; b bits, table lookup, output materialization and setup are all paid. W1 needs no bits.

With a cap B, rejection of all B independent blocks has probability δ≤2^(−B). On the extended alphabet the capped law is `(1−δ)r_s+δ·FAIL`, whose TV from r_s is δ.
